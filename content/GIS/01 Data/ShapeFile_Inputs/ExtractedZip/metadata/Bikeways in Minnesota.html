Section 1: Overview

Originator:Minnesota Department of Transportation

Title: Bikeways in Minnesota

Abstract: This dataset shows linear bikeways throughout Minnesota. Data is gathered through annual update requests of many public partners including County and Municipal contacts, Metropolitan Planning Organizations, Regional Development Commissions and the Minnesota DNR. Updates are occasionally provided by private partners including the MTB project and consulting agencies. Comments and corrections people submit on the Minnesota Bikeways Crowdsourcing App also inform the dataset. The data is vetted and maintained by the Geographic Information Mapping unit in the MnDOT Transportation System Management Office, and staff in the Transit and Active Transportation Office of MnDOT. The initial dataset was developed in partnership with the Minnesota Geospatial Advisory Council, and the Minnesota Geospatial Information Office.

Purpose: The purpose of this data set is to provide a single, commonly accepted set of attribute specifications (bikeway name, type, and length) for aggregating and mapping bikeways data in Minnesota. It will improve the ability to share data resources between parties by reducing incompatibilities when acquiring, processing, storing, and disseminating bikeways data. This data set will be useful to local, regional, and state governments for identifying network gaps within and between jurisdictions, easing data transfer between all levels of government and across partnering stakeholder organizations, and – in context with other feature classes / shapefiles – providing data critical for level of service, level of traffic stress and other network evaluations.

Section 2: Data Quality

Attribute Accuracy: Data is as accurate as what we receive from our partners. Data is sometimes sent to MnDOT in the Bikeways Data Standard and other times we re-format it to comply with the standard.

Logical Consistency: All features in this Bikeways dataset have been validated to assure they meet the Bikeways Data Standard attribute specifications and domains.

Completeness: MnDOT requests annual updates from many public partners including County and Municipal contacts, Metropolitan Planning Organizations, Regional Development Commissions and the Minnesota DNR. Updates are occasionally provided by private partners including the MTB project and some consulting agencies. In addition, anyone at anytime can submit comments and corrections through the crowdsource reporter. All these data segments are vetted by MnDOT staff before they are added to the bikeways dataset.

Horizontal Positional Accuracy: This dataset is best suited for general reference. Data has been appended from multiple sources which have varying levels of accuracy. A portion of the data has been georeferenced and digitized using aerial photography. There is a continual effort to improve data quality and completeness. Each addition is a step toward the long-term goal of having a complete statewide bikeways layer for ongoing use by Minnesota's GIS community.

Vertical Positional Accuracy: Not Applicable

Lineage: MnDOT has regularly requested and collected bikeways data from local, regional and state partners since the 1990s for the creation of the Statewide Bicycle Maps. Since 2013, MnDOT has compiled, maintained, published, and distributed a map of bikeways every other year in compliance with Minnesota State Statute 160.262. Initially, updates were primarily submitted as hand drawn lines on paper or written explanations of bikeway features and placements. In the 2010s, the bikeways map was converted from paper to CADD which made sharing status and receiving updates easier. In 2018, the bikeways map was converted to GIS, and MnDOT's LRS team began supporting and maintaining bikeway information (including road shoulder surface type and widths). In 2021, the Bikeways Data Standard was developed in partnership with the MnGAC and MnGEO, and work began to convert all historically relevant data into the standard which improved mapping and management abilities. This lead to the release of the Bikeways web-based map in 2021, which is the most complete and expansive Minnesota bikeways map yet.
